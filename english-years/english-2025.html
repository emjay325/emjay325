Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

SECTION 1: ANTONYMS

Choose the word or group of words opposite in meaning to the underlined word.

Question 1

The principal is very rigid in his approach to discipline.

  • A. flexible
  • B. harsh
  • C. strict
  • D. strong
Answer: A. flexible
Explanation: Rigid means stiff or unyielding; the opposite is flexible or adaptable.

Question 2

The soldiers were praised for their gallant behavior.

  • A. brave
  • B. cowardly
  • C. polite
  • D. reckless
Answer: B. cowardly
Explanation: Gallant means brave and heroic; the opposite is cowardly.

Question 3

His speech was explicit on the matter of funding.

  • A. clear
  • B. vague
  • C. loud
  • D. honest
Answer: B. vague
Explanation: Explicit means stated clearly and in detail; the opposite is vague or unclear.

Question 4

The company recorded a surplus this financial year.

  • A. profit
  • B. loss
  • C. deficit
  • D. balance
Answer: C. deficit
Explanation: Surplus means an amount left over; the opposite (especially in finance) is a deficit.

Question 5

She was optimistic about the outcome of the interview.

  • A. hopeful
  • B. cheerful
  • C. pessimistic
  • D. indifferent
Answer: C. pessimistic
Explanation: Optimistic means hopeful about the future; the opposite is pessimistic.

Question 6

The manager's comments were offensive to the staff.

  • A. insulting
  • B. flattering
  • C. polite
  • D. rude
Answer: C. polite
Explanation: Offensive means causing someone to feel resentful or annoyed; the opposite is polite or pleasant.

Question 7

The witness was certain about the identity of the thief.

  • A. doubtful
  • B. sure
  • C. convinced
  • D. happy
Answer: A. doubtful
Explanation: Certain means firm conviction; the opposite is doubtful or uncertain.

Question 8

The path was straight and easy to follow.

  • A. narrow
  • B. crooked
  • C. wide
  • D. long
Answer: B. crooked
Explanation: Straight means without curves or bends; the opposite is crooked or winding.

Question 9

The king was known for his arrogance.

  • A. pride
  • B. wealth
  • C. humility
  • D. power
Answer: C. humility
Explanation: Arrogance means an exaggerated sense of one's own importance; the opposite is humility.

Question 10

The noise in the room was transient.

  • A. temporary
  • B. permanent
  • C. loud
  • D. sudden
Answer: B. permanent
Explanation: Transient means lasting only for a short time; the opposite is permanent.

SECTION 2: SYNONYMS

Choose the word or group of words nearest in meaning to the underlined word.

Question 11

The lawyer gave a lucid explanation of the case.

  • A. complicated
  • B. clear
  • C. long
  • D. boring
Answer: B. clear
Explanation: Lucid means expressed clearly or easy to understand.

Question 12

He is an affable young man.

  • A. friendly
  • B. rude
  • C. wealthy
  • D. clever
Answer: A. friendly
Explanation: Affable means friendly, good-natured, or easy to talk to.

Question 13

The precarious state of the economy is a concern for all.

  • A. stable
  • B. uncertain
  • C. wealthy
  • D. improving
Answer: B. uncertain
Explanation: Precarious means not securely held or in position; dangerously likely to fall or collapse.

Question 14

She is a meticulous worker.

  • A. lazy
  • B. careful
  • C. fast
  • D. messy
Answer: B. careful
Explanation: Meticulous means showing great attention to detail; very careful and precise.

Question 15

The dictator tried to coerce the people into obeying him.

  • A. help
  • B. force
  • C. invite
  • D. teach
Answer: B. force
Explanation: Coerce means to persuade an unwilling person to do something by using force or threats.

Question 16

The athlete felt lethargic after the long race.

  • A. energetic
  • B. sluggish
  • C. hungry
  • D. happy
Answer: B. sluggish
Explanation: Lethargic means affected by lethargy; sluggish and apathetic.

Question 17

His candid remarks surprised everyone at the meeting.

  • A. dishonest
  • B. frank
  • C. secret
  • D. long
Answer: B. frank
Explanation: Candid means truthful and straightforward; frank.

Question 18

The outcome of the match was inevitable.

  • A. surprising
  • B. unavoidable
  • C. doubtful
  • D. strange
Answer: B. unavoidable
Explanation: Inevitable means certain to happen; unavoidable.

Question 19

She has an amiable personality.

  • A. difficult
  • B. pleasant
  • C. cold
  • D. aggressive
Answer: B. pleasant
Explanation: Amiable means having or displaying a friendly and pleasant manner.

Question 20

The student was very loquacious during the break.

  • A. quiet
  • B. talkative
  • C. serious
  • D. studious
Answer: B. talkative
Explanation: Loquacious means tending to talk a great deal; talkative.

SECTION 3: IDIOMS AND INTERPRETATION

Choose the most appropriate interpretation for the underlined idiomatic expression.

Question 21

After the long argument, they decided to bury the hatchet.

  • A. hide a weapon
  • B. end a quarrel
  • C. start a new fight
  • D. dig a hole
Answer: B. end a quarrel
Explanation: To bury the hatchet means to make peace and end a conflict.

Question 22

The witness's story was a bit thin.

  • A. short
  • B. unconvincing
  • C. interesting
  • D. very clear
Answer: B. unconvincing
Explanation: If a story is "thin," it lacks supporting evidence and is hard to believe.

Question 23

The exam was a piece of cake for John.

  • A. very sweet
  • B. very easy
  • C. very difficult
  • D. very long
Answer: B. very easy
Explanation: A piece of cake refers to a task that is very simple to accomplish.

Question 24

I am feeling under the weather today.

  • A. feeling cold
  • B. feeling unwell
  • C. feeling happy
  • D. feeling wet
Answer: B. feeling unwell
Explanation: To be under the weather means to feel sick or slightly ill.

Question 25

The lawyer hit the nail on the head with his argument.

  • A. was physically aggressive
  • B. said exactly the right thing
  • C. made a mistake
  • D. failed to speak
Answer: B. said exactly the right thing
Explanation: To hit the nail on the head means to describe exactly what is causing a situation or problem.

Question 26

That luxury car cost an arm and a leg.

  • A. was very dangerous
  • B. was very expensive
  • C. was a gift
  • D. was very cheap
Answer: B. was very expensive
Explanation: An arm and a leg refers to something that is extremely pricey.

Question 27

Who let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party?

  • A. released a pet
  • B. revealed a secret
  • C. bought a cat
  • D. made a mess
Answer: B. revealed a secret
Explanation: To let the cat out of the bag means to reveal a secret, usually by accident.

Question 28

The two friends stood by each other through thick and thin.

  • A. only in good times
  • B. in both good and bad times
  • C. through narrow paths
  • D. while eating together
Answer: B. in both good and bad times
Explanation: Through thick and thin means despite all difficulties or changes in fortune.

Question 29

Stop beating around the bush and tell me the truth.

  • A. hitting trees
  • B. avoiding the main point
  • C. cleaning the garden
  • D. speaking too loudly
Answer: B. avoiding the main point
Explanation: Beating around the bush means discussing a matter without coming to the point.

Question 30

I know he spilled the beans to the teacher.

  • A. cooked food
  • B. gave away a secret
  • C. made a mistake
  • D. wasted time
Answer: B. gave away a secret
Explanation: To spill the beans means to reveal information that was meant to be private.

SECTION 4: GRAMMAR AND STRUCTURE

Choose the word or group of words that best completes the sentence.

Question 31

The boy, as well as his sisters, ______ coming to the party tonight.

  • A. are
  • B. is
  • C. were
  • D. have been
Answer: B. is
Explanation: When a subject is followed by "as well as," the verb agrees with the primary subject (The boy), which is singular.

Question 32

I ______ my breakfast before he arrived at the house.

  • A. have eaten
  • B. had eaten
  • C. ate
  • D. was eating
Answer: B. had eaten
Explanation: Use the past perfect tense (had eaten) to show one past action happened before another past action (arrived).

Question 33

If I ______ you, I would accept the offer immediately.

  • A. am
  • B. was
  • C. were
  • D. are
Answer: C. were
Explanation: This is a second conditional sentence. In hypothetical "if" clauses, "were" is used for all persons.

Question 34

Neither the students nor the teacher ______ the answer to the question.

  • A. knows
  • B. know
  • C. is knowing
  • D. have known
Answer: A. knows
Explanation: In "neither/nor" structures, the verb agrees with the closer subject (the teacher), which is singular.

Question 35

The man ______ I saw yesterday is a famous doctor.

  • A. who
  • B. whom
  • C. which
  • D. whose
Answer: B. whom
Explanation: "Whom" is the objective relative pronoun used because the man is the object of the verb "saw."

Question 36

You have finished the work, ______?

  • A. haven't you
  • B. have you
  • C. didn't you
  • D. you have
Answer: A. haven't you
Explanation: A positive statement in the present perfect tense requires a negative question tag.

Question 37

She is fond ______ listening to classical music.

  • A. for
  • B. with
  • C. of
  • D. at
Answer: C. of
Explanation: The adjective "fond" is always paired with the preposition "of."

Question 38

The heavy rain prevented us ______ going to the stadium.

  • A. to
  • B. from
  • C. with
  • D. in
Answer: B. from
Explanation: The verb "prevent" is followed by the preposition "from" and an -ing verb.

Question 39

He is the ______ of the two brothers.

  • A. tallest
  • B. taller
  • C. tall
  • D. more tall
Answer: B. taller
Explanation: When comparing only two people or things, the comparative degree (-er) is used.

Question 40

The news ______ so shocking that she fainted.

  • A. are
  • B. were
  • C. is
  • D. was
Answer: D. was
Explanation: "News" is an uncountable noun and takes a singular verb. "Was" is used here for the past context.

Question 41

It's time we ______ for the airport.

  • A. leave
  • B. left
  • C. should leave
  • D. are leaving
Answer: B. left
Explanation: After the phrase "It's time" (meaning the time is already past), we use the past tense of the verb.

Question 42

I didn't see ______ at the party.

  • A. no one
  • B. anyone
  • C. someone
  • D. nobody
Answer: B. anyone
Explanation: In negative sentences, "anyone" is used instead of "someone" or "no one" (to avoid double negatives).

Question 43

She ______ the house for two hours before she realized she lost her keys.

  • A. was cleaning
  • B. had been cleaning
  • C. cleaned
  • D. has been cleaning
Answer: B. had been cleaning
Explanation: The past perfect continuous (had been -ing) describes an action that was ongoing until another past event occurred.

Question 44

Which ______ these books belongs to you?

  • A. from
  • B. in
  • C. of
  • D. between
Answer: C. of
Explanation: "Which of" is the standard phrase used to ask about a specific selection from a group.

Question 45

You ______ smoke in the hospital; it is forbidden.

  • A. wouldn't
  • B. mustn't
  • C. needn't
  • D. won't
Answer: B. mustn't
Explanation: "Mustn't" expresses a strong prohibition or rule.

Question 46

He is looking forward ______ his graduation ceremony next month.

  • A. to attend
  • B. to attending
  • C. attending
  • D. for attending
Answer: B. to attending
Explanation: The phrasal verb "look forward to" requires a gerund (-ing) after the preposition "to."

Question 47

The students ______ their teacher's instructions carefully.

  • A. listened
  • B. heard
  • C. followed
  • D. obeyed to
Answer: C. followed
Explanation: "Followed instructions" is the most idiomatic and grammatically correct way to describe acting on guidance.

Question 48

I would have helped you if you ______ me.

  • A. ask
  • B. asked
  • C. had asked
  • D. have asked
Answer: C. had asked
Explanation: This is a third conditional (hypothetical past). The "if" clause uses the past perfect (had asked).

Question 49

Hardly ______ the stage when the crowd began to cheer.

  • A. he had reached
  • B. had he reached
  • C. did he reach
  • D. he reached
Answer: B. had he reached
Explanation: When a sentence begins with a negative adverb like "Hardly," the subject and auxiliary verb are inverted (had he).

Question 50

Each of the three suspects ______ interrogated by the police.

  • A. was
  • B. were
  • C. have been
  • D. are being
Answer: A. was
Explanation: "Each" is always a singular subject and therefore takes the singular verb "was."
