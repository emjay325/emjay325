Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

SECTION 1: ANTONYMS

Choose the word or group of words opposite in meaning to the underlined word.

Question 1

The principal is very rigid in his approach to discipline.

  • A. flexible
  • B. harsh
  • C. strict
  • D. strong
Answer: A. flexible
Explanation: Rigid means stiff or unyielding; the opposite is flexible or adaptable.

Question 2

The soldiers were praised for their gallant behavior during the rescue.

  • A. brave
  • B. cowardly
  • C. polite
  • D. reckless
Answer: B. cowardly
Explanation: Gallant means brave and heroic; the opposite is cowardly.

Question 3

His speech was explicit on the matter of how the money was spent.

  • A. clear
  • B. vague
  • C. loud
  • D. honest
Answer: B. vague
Explanation: Explicit means stated clearly and in detail; the opposite is vague or unclear.

Question 4

The local government recorded a surplus this financial year.

  • A. profit
  • B. loss
  • C. deficit
  • D. balance
Answer: C. deficit
Explanation: Surplus means an amount left over; the opposite in finance is a deficit.

Question 5

She was optimistic about the outcome of the job interview.

  • A. hopeful
  • B. cheerful
  • C. pessimistic
  • D. indifferent
Answer: C. pessimistic
Explanation: Optimistic means hopeful about the future; the opposite is pessimistic.

Question 6

The manager's comments were offensive to the hardworking staff.

  • A. insulting
  • B. flattering
  • C. polite
  • D. rude
Answer: C. polite
Explanation: Offensive means causing resentment; the opposite is polite or complimentary.

Question 7

The witness was certain about the identity of the intruder.

  • A. doubtful
  • B. sure
  • C. convinced
  • D. happy
Answer: A. doubtful
Explanation: Certain means firm conviction; the opposite is doubtful or unsure.

Question 8

The old path through the woods was straight.

  • A. narrow
  • B. crooked
  • C. wide
  • D. long
Answer: B. crooked
Explanation: Straight means direct; the opposite is crooked, winding, or curved.

Question 9

The king was known throughout the land for his arrogance.

  • A. pride
  • B. wealth
  • C. humility
  • D. power
Answer: C. humility
Explanation: Arrogance means exaggerated self-importance; the opposite is humility.

Question 10

The sharp pain he felt in his chest was transient.

  • A. temporary
  • B. permanent
  • C. loud
  • D. sudden
Answer: B. permanent
Explanation: Transient means lasting for a short time; the opposite is permanent or enduring.

SECTION 2: SYNONYMS

Choose the word or group of words nearest in meaning to the underlined word.

Question 11

The lawyer gave a lucid explanation of the complex case.

  • A. complicated
  • B. clear
  • C. long
  • D. boring
Answer: B. clear
Explanation: Lucid means expressed clearly and easy to understand.

Question 12

Everyone enjoys his company because he is an affable young man.

  • A. friendly
  • B. rude
  • C. wealthy
  • D. clever
Answer: A. friendly
Explanation: Affable means friendly, good-natured, and easy to talk to.

Question 13

The precarious state of the nation's economy is a concern for all.

  • A. stable
  • B. uncertain
  • C. wealthy
  • D. improving
Answer: B. uncertain
Explanation: Precarious means not securely held; dangerously likely to fall or collapse.

Question 14

The accountant is known to be a meticulous worker.

  • A. lazy
  • B. careful
  • C. fast
  • D. messy
Answer: B. careful
Explanation: Meticulous means showing great attention to detail; very careful and precise.

Question 15

The security guards tried to coerce the witness into changing his story.

  • A. help
  • B. force
  • C. invite
  • D. teach
Answer: B. force
Explanation: Coerce means to persuade or pressure someone to do something using force or threats.

Question 16

The workers felt lethargic after the heavy afternoon meal.

  • A. energetic
  • B. sluggish
  • C. hungry
  • D. happy
Answer: B. sluggish
Explanation: Lethargic means sluggish, apathetic, or lacking energy.

Question 17

His candid remarks about the project surprised the board members.

  • A. dishonest
  • B. frank
  • C. secret
  • D. long
Answer: B. frank
Explanation: Candid means truthful, straightforward, and frank.

Question 18

With such poor preparation, the defeat of the team was inevitable.

  • A. surprising
  • B. unavoidable
  • C. doubtful
  • D. strange
Answer: B. unavoidable
Explanation: Inevitable means certain to happen and unavoidable.

Question 19

The new nurse has an amiable personality.

  • A. difficult
  • B. pleasant
  • C. cold
  • D. aggressive
Answer: B. pleasant
Explanation: Amiable means having a friendly and pleasant manner.

Question 20

The student was very loquacious during the long bus ride.

  • A. quiet
  • B. talkative
  • C. serious
  • D. studious
Answer: B. talkative
Explanation: Loquacious means tending to talk a great deal; talkative.

SECTION 3: IDIOMS AND INTERPRETATION

Choose the most appropriate interpretation for the idiomatic expression.

Question 21

After the long family feud, the brothers finally decided to bury the hatchet.

  • A. hide their weapons
  • B. end their quarrel
  • C. start a new fight
  • D. buy new tools
Answer: B. end their quarrel
Explanation: To bury the hatchet means to make peace and end a conflict.

Question 22

The excuse he gave for coming late to school was a bit thin.

  • A. very short
  • B. unconvincing
  • C. interesting
  • D. simple to understand
Answer: B. unconvincing
Explanation: If an excuse or story is "thin," it lacks substance and is hard to believe.

Question 23

Since she had studied all night, the exam was a piece of cake for her.

  • A. very sweet
  • B. very easy
  • C. very difficult
  • D. a pleasant surprise
Answer: B. very easy
Explanation: "A piece of cake" refers to something that is very simple or easy to do.

Question 24

I cannot come to work today because I am feeling under the weather.

  • A. feeling cold
  • B. feeling unwell
  • C. feeling happy
  • D. stuck in the rain
Answer: B. feeling unwell
Explanation: To be "under the weather" means to feel sick or slightly ill.

Question 25

The detective hit the nail on the head with his theory about the robbery.

  • A. injured himself
  • B. was exactly right
  • C. made a big mistake
  • D. spoke too loudly
Answer: B. was exactly right
Explanation: To hit the nail on the head means to state exactly the right answer or cause of a situation.

Question 26

Repairing the engine of that vintage car will cost an arm and a leg.

  • A. result in an injury
  • B. be very expensive
  • C. be a quick job
  • D. require manual labor
Answer: B. be very expensive
Explanation: "An arm and a leg" refers to a very high price or cost.

Question 27

I wonder who let the cat out of the bag about our secret plans.

  • A. lost a pet
  • B. revealed a secret
  • C. made a mistake
  • D. opened the door
Answer: B. revealed a secret
Explanation: To "let the cat out of the bag" means to reveal a secret, often accidentally.

Question 28

The two friends stayed together through thick and thin.

  • A. despite all difficulties
  • B. only when times were good
  • C. in narrow places
  • D. while traveling
Answer: A. despite all difficulties
Explanation: "Through thick and thin" means to remain supportive despite all difficulties or hardships.

Question 29

I wish you would stop beating around the bush and tell me the problem.

  • A. wasting time in the garden
  • B. avoiding the main issue
  • C. hitting the plants
  • D. talking too softly
Answer: B. avoiding the main issue
Explanation: "Beating around the bush" means to avoid talking about what is important.

Question 30

I Suspect he spilled the beans to the authorities about the plan.

  • A. wasted his food
  • B. gave away the secret
  • C. committed a crime
  • D. lied about the plan
Answer: B. gave away the secret
Explanation: To spill the beans means to reveal information that was intended to be secret.

SECTION 4: GRAMMAR AND STRUCTURE

Choose the word or group of words that best completes the sentence.

Question 31

The boy, together with his friends, ______ expected at the party.

  • A. are
  • B. is
  • C. were
  • D. have been
Answer: B. is
Explanation: When a singular subject is followed by "together with," the verb agrees with the primary subject (The boy).

Question 32

I ______ my lunch before he arrived at my office.

  • A. have eaten
  • B. had eaten
  • C. ate
  • D. was eating
Answer: B. had eaten
Explanation: The past perfect tense (had eaten) is used for an action completed before another past action (arrived).

Question 33

If I ______ a rich man, I would travel around the world.

  • A. am
  • B. was
  • C. were
  • D. are
Answer: C. were
Explanation: This is a second conditional sentence (hypothetical). "Were" is used for all persons in the if-clause.

Question 34

Neither the students nor their teacher ______ the reason for the delay.

  • A. knows
  • B. know
  • C. is knowing
  • D. have known
Answer: A. knows
Explanation: In "neither/nor" structures, the verb agrees with the closer subject (the teacher), which is singular.

Question 35

The woman ______ I spoke to this morning is my aunt.

  • A. who
  • B. whom
  • C. which
  • D. whose
Answer: B. whom
Explanation: "Whom" is used as the object relative pronoun when referring to people.

Question 36

You haven't finished your homework yet, ______?

  • A. haven't you
  • B. have you
  • C. didn't you
  • D. had you
Answer: B. have you
Explanation: A negative statement takes a positive question tag.

Question 37

The young girl is fond ______ playing with dolls.

  • A. for
  • B. with
  • C. of
  • D. at
Answer: C. of
Explanation: The adjective "fond" is always followed by the preposition "of."

Question 38

The heavy rain prevented the match ______ starting on time.

  • A. to
  • B. from
  • C. with
  • D. in
Answer: B. from
Explanation: The verb "prevent" is followed by the preposition "from" and a gerund.

Question 39

Between the two cars, the red one is the ______.

  • A. fastest
  • B. faster
  • C. fast
  • D. more fast
Answer: B. faster
Explanation: When comparing only two items, the comparative degree (-er) is used.

Question 40

The news about the accident ______ very distressing.

  • A. are
  • B. were
  • C. is
  • D. was
Answer: D. was
Explanation: "News" is an uncountable noun that takes a singular verb. "Was" is the correct past tense form.

Question 41

It is time the children ______ to bed.

  • A. go
  • B. went
  • C. should go
  • D. are going
Answer: B. went
Explanation: After the phrase "It is time" (high time), the past tense is used for a present situation.

Question 42

I didn't see ______ in the dark room.

  • A. no one
  • B. anyone
  • C. someone
  • D. nobody
Answer: B. anyone
Explanation: In negative sentences, "anyone" is used instead of "someone" or "no one."

Question 43

The students ______ for the bus for an hour before it arrived.

  • A. were waiting
  • B. had been waiting
  • C. waited
  • D. have been waiting
Answer: B. had been waiting
Explanation: The past perfect continuous (had been -ing) describes an action that was ongoing until another point in the past.

Question 44

Can you tell me which ______ these bags is yours?

  • A. from
  • B. in
  • C. of
  • D. between
Answer: C. of
Explanation: "Which of" is the correct standard construction for selection from a group.

Question 45

Visitors ______ walk on the grass; it is strictly prohibited.

  • A. wouldn't
  • B. mustn't
  • C. needn't
  • D. won't
Answer: B. mustn't
Explanation: "Mustn't" is used to express a prohibition.

Question 46

He is looking forward ______ his long-awaited vacation.

  • A. to start
  • B. to starting
  • C. starting
  • D. for starting
Answer: B. to starting
Explanation: The phrase "look forward to" is followed by a gerund (-ing form).

Question 47

The candidate was advised to ______ the examiner's instructions.

  • A. listen
  • B. hear
  • C. follow
  • D. obey to
Answer: C. follow
Explanation: "Follow instructions" is the standard idiomatic expression.

Question 48

I would have given you the book if you ______ me.

  • A. ask
  • B. asked
  • C. had asked
  • D. have asked
Answer: C. had asked
Explanation: This is a third conditional (hypothetical past). The structure is "if + past perfect."

Question 49

Hardly ______ the building when the explosion occurred.

  • A. he had entered
  • B. had he entered
  • C. did he enter
  • D. he entered
Answer: B. had he entered
Explanation: Sentences starting with negative adverbs like "Hardly" require inversion of the subject and auxiliary verb.

Question 50

Each of the candidates ______ to be interviewed by the panel.

  • A. was
  • B. were
  • C. have been
  • D. are being
Answer: A. was
Explanation: "Each" is always a singular pronoun and requires a singular verb.
