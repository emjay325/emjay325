Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

Question 1

A figure of speech in which a part is used to represent the whole is called

  • A. Metonymy
  • B. Synecdoche
  • C. Oxymoron
  • D. Personification
Answer: B. Synecdoche
Explanation: Synecdoche is a figure of speech where a part stands for the whole, such as using wheels to mean a car.

Question 2

The substitution of the name of an attribute or adjunct for that of the thing meant is

  • A. Metonymy
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Simile
  • D. Irony
Answer: A. Metonymy
Explanation: Metonymy involves using a related concept to represent something, like using the Crown to refer to a monarch.

Question 3

A narrative technique that records the multi layered thoughts and feelings of a character is

  • A. Omniscient narration
  • B. Stream of consciousness
  • C. Flashback
  • D. First person point of view
Answer: B. Stream of consciousness
Explanation: This technique attempts to portray the continuous flow of thoughts in a character mind.

Question 4

A poem that tells a story and is often meant to be sung is a

  • A. Lyric
  • B. Ballad
  • C. Sonnet
  • D. Ode
Answer: B. Ballad
Explanation: Ballads are narrative poems with a song like quality, often focusing on themes of love or adventure.

Question 5

In drama, the words spoken by a character directly to the audience, unheard by others on stage, is an

  • A. Aside
  • B. Soliloquy
  • C. Monologue
  • D. Epilogue
Answer: A. Aside
Explanation: An aside is a brief remark made by a character to the audience while other characters are present but unaware.

Question 6

A literary work that pokes fun at another work or its style is a

  • A. Satire
  • B. Parody
  • C. Comedy
  • D. Farce
Answer: B. Parody
Explanation: Parody imitates the style of a particular writer or genre with deliberate exaggeration for comic effect.

Question 7

A play characterized by broad humor, wild antics, and physical comedy is a

  • A. Tragedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Melodrama
  • D. Tragi comedy
Answer: B. Farce
Explanation: Farce relies on exaggerated situations and physical humor rather than character development.

Question 8

The continuation of a sentence without a pause beyond the end of a line or stanza is

  • A. Enjambment
  • B. Caesura
  • C. Alliteration
  • D. Onomatopoeia
Answer: A. Enjambment
Explanation: Enjambment occurs when a poetic line does not end with punctuation but flows into the next line.

Question 9

Which figure of speech is used in the phrase The pen is mightier than the sword?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Metonymy
  • C. Personification
  • D. Hyperbole
Answer: B. Metonymy
Explanation: The pen represents writing or diplomacy, while the sword represents military force.

Question 10

A character who undergoes a significant internal change during the course of a story is a

  • A. Static character
  • B. Dynamic character
  • C. Flat character
  • D. Stock character
Answer: B. Dynamic character
Explanation: Dynamic characters evolve in terms of personality or perspective due to the events in the plot.

Question 11

The error of judgment or character flaw that leads to the downfall of a tragic hero is

  • A. Catharsis
  • B. Hubris
  • C. Hamartia
  • D. Anagnorisis
Answer: C. Hamartia
Explanation: Hamartia is the Greek term for the fatal flaw that causes a protagonist ruin.

Question 12

A poem of nineteen lines with a highly structured repeat of lines is a

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Villanelle
  • C. Haiku
  • D. Sestina
Answer: B. Villanelle
Explanation: A villanelle has five tercets and a final quatrain with two lines repeating throughout.

Question 13

The use of an understatement to emphasize a point, often using a double negative, is

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Hyperbole
  • C. Irony
  • D. Paradox
Answer: A. Litotes
Explanation: An example of litotes is saying He is not a bad singer to mean he is a good one.

Question 14

A recurring image, sound, action, or other figure that has a symbolic significance in a story is a

  • A. Theme
  • B. Motif
  • C. Plot
  • D. Setting
Answer: B. Motif
Explanation: A motif helps develop the theme of a literary work through repetition.

Question 15

A short story that uses animal characters to teach a moral lesson is a

  • A. Myth
  • B. Legend
  • C. Fable
  • D. Parable
Answer: C. Fable
Explanation: Fables typically feature anthropomorphized animals and end with a clear moral.

Question 16

The release of built up emotional tension by the audience at the end of a tragedy is

  • A. Hamartia
  • B. Hubris
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Climax
Answer: C. Catharsis
Explanation: Catharsis is the purification or cleansing of emotions like pity and fear.

Question 17

Which figure of speech is seen in Parting is such sweet sorrow?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Oxymoron
  • C. Personification
  • D. Hyperbole
Answer: B. Oxymoron
Explanation: Sweet and sorrow are contradictory terms placed together for effect.

Question 18

The dictionary definition of a word is its

  • A. Connotation
  • B. Denotation
  • C. Syntax
  • D. Diction
Answer: B. Denotation
Explanation: Denotation is the literal meaning, while connotation refers to emotional associations.

Question 19

A type of novel that tells its story through a series of documents or letters is

  • A. Picaresque
  • B. Epistolary
  • C. Gothic
  • D. Allegorical
Answer: B. Epistolary
Explanation: An epistolary novel uses letters, diary entries, or newspaper clippings to advance the plot.

Question 20

A break or pause in the middle of a line of poetry is a

  • A. Enjambment
  • B. Caesura
  • C. Foot
  • D. Meter
Answer: B. Caesura
Explanation: A caesura is a rhythmic pause often indicated by punctuation within a line.

Question 21

The sequence of events in a literary work is the

  • A. Plot
  • B. Setting
  • C. Theme
  • D. Tone
Answer: A. Plot
Explanation: Plot is the organized pattern or sequence of events that make up the story.

Question 22

A character who represents a single personality trait and does not change is a

  • A. Round character
  • B. Flat character
  • C. Dynamic character
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: B. Flat character
Explanation: Flat characters are two dimensional and uncomplicated, often used as background or stock characters.

Question 23

The narrator voice in a poem is referred to as the

  • A. Author
  • B. Persona
  • C. Character
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: B. Persona
Explanation: The persona is the speaker created by the poet to tell the poem.

Question 24

A comic imitation of a serious literary type that treats a high subject in a low way is

  • A. Parody
  • B. Burlesque
  • C. Satire
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Burlesque
Explanation: Burlesque focuses on the mismatch between style and subject matter for humor.

Question 25

The time of year, day, or weather in a story is part of the

  • A. Plot
  • B. Conflict
  • C. Setting
  • D. Exposition
Answer: C. Setting
Explanation: Setting encompasses the physical location and the temporal context of the narrative.

Question 26

A reference to a statement, person, place, or event from literature, history, or mythology is an

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Allegory
  • D. Analogy
Answer: B. Allusion
Explanation: Allusions allow authors to add layers of meaning without detailed explanation.

Question 27

Which of the following describes the rhythmic structure of a verse?

  • A. Meter
  • B. Stanza
  • C. Rhyme
  • D. Couplet
Answer: A. Meter
Explanation: Meter is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of poetry.

Question 28

A poem that praises a person, object, or event is an

  • A. Elegy
  • B. Ode
  • C. Lyric
  • D. Sonnet
Answer: B. Ode
Explanation: Odes are serious and lyrical poems written in celebration or praise.

Question 29

In literature, a symbol is something that

  • A. Explains the plot
  • B. Represents something beyond itself
  • C. Describes the setting
  • D. Ends the story
Answer: B. Represents something beyond itself
Explanation: A symbol uses a concrete object or action to suggest abstract ideas.

Question 30

When the audience knows something that the characters in a play do not, it is

  • A. Verbal irony
  • B. Situational irony
  • C. Dramatic irony
  • D. Sarcasm
Answer: C. Dramatic irony
Explanation: Dramatic irony creates tension because the audience anticipates consequences the characters are unaware of.

Question 31

A long speech by one character in a play, directed to other characters, is a

  • A. Soliloquy
  • B. Monologue
  • C. Aside
  • D. Dialogue
Answer: B. Monologue
Explanation: Unlike a soliloquy, a monologue is heard by other characters in the scene.

Question 32

Which figure of speech is used in The smiling sun looked down on us?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Simile
  • C. Personification
  • D. Oxymoron
Answer: C. Personification
Explanation: Attributing the human action of smiling to the sun is personification.

Question 33

The resolution or final outcome of the plot in a comedy is the

  • A. Climax
  • B. Denouement
  • C. Exposition
  • D. Catastrophe
Answer: B. Denouement
Explanation: Denouement is the final part where the strands of the plot are drawn together.

Question 34

A poem with no fixed rhyme or regular meter is called

  • A. Blank verse
  • B. Free verse
  • C. Rhyming verse
  • D. Lyric
Answer: B. Free verse
Explanation: Free verse does not follow traditional poetic rules of structure or rhythm.

Question 35

Which literary device uses words to convey the opposite of their literal meaning?

  • A. Irony
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Simile
  • D. Hyperbole
Answer: A. Irony
Explanation: Irony highlights the difference between appearance and reality or expectations and outcomes.

Question 36

The point of view where the narrator knows the thoughts and feelings of all characters is

  • A. First person
  • B. Third person limited
  • C. Third person omniscient
  • D. Second person
Answer: C. Third person omniscient
Explanation: An omniscient narrator is all knowing and can describe any character internal state.

Question 37

A six line stanza in poetry is called a

  • A. Quatrain
  • B. Sestet
  • C. Octave
  • D. Tercet
Answer: B. Sestet
Explanation: A sestet often forms the second part of an Italian (Petrarchan) sonnet.

Question 38

A novel about the growth and education of a protagonist from youth to adulthood is a

  • A. Picaresque novel
  • B. Bildungsroman
  • C. Gothic novel
  • D. Historical novel
Answer: B. Bildungsroman
Explanation: This genre focuses on the psychological and moral growth of the main character.

Question 39

Which figure of speech is an intentional exaggeration for effect?

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Hyperbole
  • C. Metaphor
  • D. Synecdoche
Answer: B. Hyperbole
Explanation: Hyperbole is an overstatement not meant to be taken literally, such as I am starving.

Question 40

The instructions in a play script that tell actors how to move or speak are

  • A. Dialogue
  • B. Stage directions
  • C. Prologue
  • D. Epilogue
Answer: B. Stage directions
Explanation: Stage directions provide vital information for performance that is not spoken aloud.

Question 41

An eight line stanza is called an

  • A. Octave
  • B. Sestet
  • C. Quatrain
  • D. Couplet
Answer: A. Octave
Explanation: An octave is the first part of an Italian sonnet, usually posing a problem or question.

Question 42

A character who opposes the main character in a story is the

  • A. Protagonist
  • B. Antagonist
  • C. Foil
  • D. Hero
Answer: B. Antagonist
Explanation: The antagonist creates the conflict by working against the protagonist.

Question 43

The use of words that imitate the sounds they describe is

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Onomatopoeia
  • C. Assonance
  • D. Consonance
Answer: B. Onomatopoeia
Explanation: Words like buzz or sizzle are examples of onomatopoeia.

Question 44

A type of comedy that depends on ridiculous situations and physical humor is

  • A. High comedy
  • B. Satire
  • C. Farce
  • D. Tragi comedy
Answer: C. Farce
Explanation: Farce often involves mistaken identities and exaggerated slapstick humor.

Question 45

A short introductory section of a book or play is a

  • A. Prologue
  • B. Epilogue
  • C. Appendix
  • D. Index
Answer: A. Prologue
Explanation: The prologue introduces themes or background information before the main narrative begins.

Question 46

Which of the following is a fixed 3 line poem with a 5 7 5 syllable structure?

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Haiku
  • C. Limerick
  • D. Ode
Answer: B. Haiku
Explanation: Haiku is a traditional form of Japanese poetry consisting of three unrhymed lines.

Question 47

The moral or message of a parable is often conveyed through

  • A. Complex scientific facts
  • B. Simple stories about human life
  • C. Long epic poems
  • D. Riddles
Answer: B. Simple stories about human life
Explanation: Parables are short, allegorical stories that illustrate a moral or spiritual lesson.

Question 48

In a tragedy, the moment of realization for the hero is called

  • A. Climax
  • B. Anagnorisis
  • C. Peripeteia
  • D. Catharsis
Answer: B. Anagnorisis
Explanation: Anagnorisis is the critical discovery or recognition that changes the hero perspective.

Question 49

A type of stanza consisting of two rhyming lines is a

  • A. Tercet
  • B. Quatrain
  • C. Couplet
  • D. Sestet
Answer: C. Couplet
Explanation: A couplet consists of two lines of verse, usually in the same meter and joined by rhyme.

Question 50

The use of historical events and characters in a fictional story is a

  • A. Fantasy novel
  • B. Historical novel
  • C. Science fiction
  • D. Detective novel
Answer: B. Historical novel
Explanation: Historical fiction blends factual history with invented characters and plotlines.
