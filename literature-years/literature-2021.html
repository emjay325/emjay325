Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

Question 1

The use of a humorous or ridiculous name for a person or thing is known as

  • A. Epithet
  • B. Nickname
  • C. Sobriquet
  • D. Appellation
Answer: C. Sobriquet
Explanation: A sobriquet is a descriptive name or nickname that is often humorous or affectionate.

Question 2

A narrative poem that is long and serious, usually dealing with a central hero, is an

  • A. Lyric
  • B. Epic
  • C. Ode
  • D. Ballad
Answer: B. Epic
Explanation: Epics are grand in scale and focus on the adventures of a legendary or historical figure.

Question 3

The literal or dictionary meaning of a word is its

  • A. Connotation
  • B. Denotation
  • C. Association
  • D. Definition
Answer: B. Denotation
Explanation: Denotation is the direct meaning, while connotation refers to the emotional associations.

Question 4

Which figure of speech is used in the phrase The pen is mightier than the sword?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Metonymy
  • C. Synecdoche
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Metonymy
Explanation: The pen represents the written word or diplomacy, and the sword represents military force.

Question 5

A statement that seems self contradictory but expresses a possible truth is a

  • A. Paradox
  • B. Oxymoron
  • C. Hyperbole
  • D. Litotes
Answer: A. Paradox
Explanation: A paradox is a deeper statement that holds a truth despite appearing absurd.

Question 6

The character who works against the protagonist is called the

  • A. Villain
  • B. Antagonist
  • C. Foil
  • D. Rival
Answer: B. Antagonist
Explanation: The antagonist provides the primary opposition to the main character or protagonist.

Question 7

A poem of fourteen lines with a specific rhyme scheme is a

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Haiku
  • C. Villanelle
  • D. Limerick
Answer: A. Sonnet
Explanation: The sonnet is a fixed form of poetry, often associated with Shakespeare or Petrarch.

Question 8

The technique of hinting at future events in a story is

  • A. Flashback
  • B. Foreshadowing
  • C. Suspense
  • D. Pacing
Answer: B. Foreshadowing
Explanation: Foreshadowing helps create anticipation or prepares the reader for what is to come.

Question 9

A play that ends in a happy way, often involving marriage, is a

  • A. Tragedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Comedy
  • D. Melodrama
Answer: C. Comedy
Explanation: Classical comedy is defined by its lighthearted tone and positive resolution.

Question 10

The repetition of vowel sounds in nearby words is called

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Assonance
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Rhythm
Answer: B. Assonance
Explanation: Assonance focuses on vowel sounds, whereas alliteration focuses on initial consonant sounds.

Question 11

A speech delivered by a character alone on stage to reveal their thoughts is a

  • A. Monologue
  • B. Soliloquy
  • C. Dialogue
  • D. Aside
Answer: B. Soliloquy
Explanation: Soliloquies allow the audience to hear the private inner reflections of a character.

Question 12

The perspective from which a story is told is the

  • A. Voice
  • B. Narrative technique
  • C. Point of view
  • D. Tone
Answer: C. Point of view
Explanation: Point of view determines how much the narrator knows and how the story is presented to the reader.

Question 13

Which figure of speech is used in the phrase He is a lion in battle?

  • A. Simile
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Personification
  • D. Hyperbole
Answer: B. Metaphor
Explanation: A metaphor is a direct comparison without using like or as.

Question 14

A brief story told to illustrate a moral or religious lesson is a

  • A. Fable
  • B. Myth
  • C. Parable
  • D. Legend
Answer: C. Parable
Explanation: Parables are simple human stories used to teach ethical or spiritual truths.

Question 15

The main idea or underlying message of a piece of literature is the

  • A. Plot
  • B. Motif
  • C. Theme
  • D. Summary
Answer: C. Theme
Explanation: The theme is the central topic or insight that the author wants to communicate.

Question 16

A poem that laments the death of a person is an

  • A. Ode
  • B. Elegy
  • C. Lyric
  • D. Ballad
Answer: B. Elegy
Explanation: Elegies are serious, solemn poems, often written in honor of someone who has passed away.

Question 17

Giving human characteristics to inanimate objects is

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Personification
  • C. Simile
  • D. Alliteration
Answer: B. Personification
Explanation: Personification treats non human things as if they have human feelings or actions.

Question 18

A character who provides a strong contrast to the protagonist is a

  • A. Hero
  • B. Foil
  • C. Rival
  • D. Villain
Answer: B. Foil
Explanation: A foil character highlights the protagonist qualities by being very different from them.

Question 19

The point of highest tension or excitement in a plot is the

  • A. Exposition
  • B. Rising action
  • C. Climax
  • D. Resolution
Answer: C. Climax
Explanation: The climax is the turning point of the story where the conflict reaches its peak.

Question 20

The atmosphere or emotional feeling of a work is its

  • A. Tone
  • B. Mood
  • C. Diction
  • D. Style
Answer: B. Mood
Explanation: Mood refers to the feelings the text evokes in the reader.

Question 21

An indirect reference to a famous person, place, or event is an

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Analogy
  • D. Allegory
Answer: B. Allusion
Explanation: Allusions rely on the reader knowledge of history or other works of literature.

Question 22

The arrangement of events in a story is the

  • A. Theme
  • B. Plot
  • C. Setting
  • D. Conflict
Answer: B. Plot
Explanation: Plot is the structured sequence of events that make up a narrative.

Question 23

A figure of speech in which two contradictory terms are used together is an

  • A. Paradox
  • B. Oxymoron
  • C. Hyperbole
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Oxymoron
Explanation: An oxymoron is a short phrase like jumbo shrimp or living death.

Question 24

The choice of words used by a writer is

  • A. Syntax
  • B. Diction
  • C. Style
  • D. Voice
Answer: B. Diction
Explanation: Diction refers to the specific vocabulary and language level chosen by the author.

Question 25

A type of literature where characters and events represent abstract ideas or morals is an

  • A. Allegory
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Analogy
  • D. Anecdote
Answer: A. Allegory
Explanation: In an allegory, the entire story serves as a symbol for a deeper meaning.

Question 26

A short, witty statement that expresses a clever or paradoxical thought is an

  • A. Epigram
  • B. Epitaph
  • C. Eulogy
  • D. Epic
Answer: A. Epigram
Explanation: Epigrams are concise and usually intended to be memorable or funny.

Question 27

The time and location in which a story takes place is the

  • A. Plot
  • B. Atmosphere
  • C. Setting
  • D. Background
Answer: C. Setting
Explanation: Setting provides the geographical and temporal context for the characters.

Question 28

A struggle between opposing forces in a story is

  • A. Climax
  • B. Conflict
  • C. Resolution
  • D. Plot
Answer: B. Conflict
Explanation: Conflict can be internal (within a character) or external (between characters or nature).

Question 29

A poem that tells a story and is often intended to be sung is a

  • A. Lyric
  • B. Ballad
  • C. Sonnet
  • D. Ode
Answer: B. Ballad
Explanation: Ballads are narrative poems that often focus on tragic or heroic stories.

Question 30

An intentional exaggeration for emphasis or dramatic effect is

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Hyperbole
  • C. Metaphor
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Hyperbole
Explanation: Hyperbole is an overstatement not meant to be taken literally.

Question 31

The repeating of initial consonant sounds in nearby words is

  • A. Assonance
  • B. Alliteration
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Rhythm
Answer: B. Alliteration
Explanation: Alliteration is a common poetic device used to create musicality or emphasis.

Question 32

A character who undergoes a significant internal change during a story is

  • A. Static
  • B. Dynamic
  • C. Flat
  • D. Stock
Answer: B. Dynamic
Explanation: Dynamic characters grow or evolve in response to the events of the plot.

Question 33

The resolution or final outcome of a story is the

  • A. Exposition
  • B. Climax
  • C. Denouement
  • D. Rising action
Answer: C. Denouement
Explanation: Denouement is the stage where the final mysteries are solved and the plot is concluded.

Question 34

Which figure of speech is used in the phrase The wind hissed through the grass?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Onomatopoeia
  • C. Simile
  • D. Personification
Answer: B. Onomatopoeia
Explanation: Onomatopoeia uses words that imitate the sound they describe.

Question 35

A stanza consisting of four lines is a

  • A. Couplet
  • B. Tercet
  • C. Quatrain
  • D. Sestet
Answer: C. Quatrain
Explanation: A quatrain is the most common stanza form in English poetry.

Question 36

The use of words to convey the opposite of their literal meaning is

  • A. Irony
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Simile
  • D. Hyperbole
Answer: A. Irony
Explanation: Irony highlights the difference between appearance and reality.

Question 37

A narrator who knows the thoughts and feelings of all the characters is

  • A. First person
  • B. Second person
  • C. Omniscient
  • D. Limited
Answer: C. Omniscient
Explanation: An omniscient narrator is all knowing and can see into any character mind.

Question 38

A type of irony where the audience knows something the characters do not is

  • A. Verbal irony
  • B. Situational irony
  • C. Dramatic irony
  • D. Sarcasm
Answer: C. Dramatic irony
Explanation: Dramatic irony creates suspense because the audience can anticipate outcomes the characters cannot.

Question 39

The speaker in a poem is often called the

  • A. Author
  • B. Persona
  • C. Hero
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: B. Persona
Explanation: The persona is the voice created by the poet to tell the poem.

Question 40

A serious play that ends in the death or downfall of the main character is a

  • A. Comedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Tragedy
  • D. Melodrama
Answer: C. Tragedy
Explanation: Tragedy explores profound themes of suffering and the inevitable downfall of the protagonist.

Question 41

Which literary genre uses actors to perform stories on a stage?

  • A. Prose
  • B. Poetry
  • C. Drama
  • D. Non fiction
Answer: C. Drama
Explanation: Drama is intended for theatrical performance through dialogue and action.

Question 42

A short speech directed to the audience that other characters are not meant to hear is an

  • A. Aside
  • B. Soliloquy
  • C. Monologue
  • D. Dialogue
Answer: A. Aside
Explanation: An aside is a brief comment made by a character directly to the audience for dramatic effect.

Question 43

The error of judgment or character flaw that leads to a hero downfall is

  • A. Hubris
  • B. Hamartia
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Hamartia
Explanation: Hamartia is the Greek term for the tragic flaw of a protagonist.

Question 44

The release of built up emotional tension at the end of a tragedy is

  • A. Hamartia
  • B. Hubris
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Anagnorisis
Answer: C. Catharsis
Explanation: Catharsis is the purification or cleansing of the audience emotions of pity and fear.

Question 45

A character who does not change during the course of a story is

  • A. Static
  • B. Dynamic
  • C. Round
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: A. Static
Explanation: Static characters stay the same from the beginning to the end of the narrative.

Question 46

A reference to a well known statement or person from the past is an

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Analogy
  • D. Allegory
Answer: B. Allusion
Explanation: Allusions allow an author to add meaning by drawing on the reader outside knowledge.

Question 47

The use of light and humorous scenes in a serious play is

  • A. Satire
  • B. Comic relief
  • C. Irony
  • D. Farce
Answer: B. Comic relief
Explanation: Comic relief provides the audience a brief break from intense emotional tension.

Question 48

Which figure of speech is an address to an absent person or an object?

  • A. Apostrophe
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Analogy
  • D. Allegory
Answer: A. Apostrophe
Explanation: In an apostrophe, the speaker addresses someone or something that cannot respond.

Question 49

A poem that praises a person, object, or idea is an

  • A. Elegy
  • B. Ode
  • C. Lyric
  • D. Ballad
Answer: B. Ode
Explanation: Odes are lyrical poems that celebrate or honor their subject.

Question 50

The substitution of a mild or pleasant word for a harsh or offensive one is a

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Euphemism
  • C. Oxymoron
  • D. Pun
Answer: B. Euphemism
Explanation: Euphemisms are used to soften the impact of unpleasant or sensitive topics.
