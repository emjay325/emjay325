Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

Question 1

A literary device where a word is used in a way that suggests two or more meanings is a

  • A. Pun
  • B. Paradox
  • C. Irony
  • D. Metaphor
Answer: A. Pun
Explanation: A pun is a play on words that exploits multiple meanings of a term or similar sounding words.

Question 2

The comic misuse of a word by confusing it with a similar sounding one is known as

  • A. Spoonerism
  • B. Malapropism
  • C. Euphemism
  • D. Archaism
Answer: B. Malapropism
Explanation: Malapropism involves using an incorrect word in place of a similar sounding one, often with a humorous result.

Question 3

A character who does not possess the traditional qualities of a hero is an

  • A. Antagonist
  • B. Anti hero
  • C. Villain
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: B. Anti hero
Explanation: An anti hero is a central character who lacks conventional heroic attributes like idealism or courage.

Question 4

The repetition of the same word or phrase at the beginning of successive lines is

  • A. Anaphora
  • B. Epistrophe
  • C. Alliteration
  • D. Assonance
Answer: A. Anaphora
Explanation: Anaphora is the deliberate repetition of the first part of a sentence or line for artistic effect.

Question 5

A narrative that begins in the middle of the action is said to start

  • A. In medias res
  • B. Ab initio
  • C. Post mortem
  • D. In toto
Answer: A. In medias res
Explanation: This Latin phrase means in the midst of things and describes a story starting after major events have begun.

Question 6

A character based on a common literary or social stereotype is a

  • A. Dynamic character
  • B. Stock character
  • C. Round character
  • D. Complex character
Answer: B. Stock character
Explanation: Stock characters are archetypal figures like the wicked stepmother or the absent minded professor.

Question 7

The use of vivid language to represent objects, actions, or ideas is

  • A. Diction
  • B. Imagery
  • C. Syntax
  • D. Style
Answer: B. Imagery
Explanation: Imagery appeals to the physical senses to help the reader visualize the scene.

Question 8

A poem that laments the loss of rural life or peaceful country scenes is

  • A. Epic
  • B. Pastoral
  • C. Ballad
  • D. Sonnet
Answer: B. Pastoral
Explanation: Pastoral literature deals with shepherds and rustic life, often idealizing the countryside.

Question 9

The feeling of pity or sadness evoked by a work of literature is

  • A. Logos
  • B. Ethos
  • C. Pathos
  • D. Bathos
Answer: C. Pathos
Explanation: Pathos is a quality that stirs up emotions of pity, sympathy, and sorrow.

Question 10

A sudden transition from the sublime or serious to the ridiculous is

  • A. Pathos
  • B. Bathos
  • C. Irony
  • D. Satire
Answer: B. Bathos
Explanation: Bathos is often unintentional and results in a failed attempt at being serious or grand.

Question 11

The use of words with the same final consonant sounds but different vowel sounds is

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Assonance
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Rhyme
Answer: C. Consonance
Explanation: Consonance refers to repetitive sounds produced by consonants within a sentence or phrase.

Question 12

An unexpected power or event saving a seemingly hopeless situation in a play is a

  • A. Denouement
  • B. Deus ex machina
  • C. Climax
  • D. Prologue
Answer: B. Deus ex machina
Explanation: This term refers to a plot device where an unsolvable problem is suddenly resolved by an improbable occurrence.

Question 13

A long narrative poem that tells the adventures of a larger than life hero is an

  • A. Lyric
  • B. Epic
  • C. Ode
  • D. Sonnet
Answer: B. Epic
Explanation: Epics involve vast settings and superhuman deeds, such as Milton Paradise Lost.

Question 14

The unrhymed form of iambic pentameter is known as

  • A. Free verse
  • B. Blank verse
  • C. Heroic couplet
  • D. Terza rima
Answer: B. Blank verse
Explanation: Blank verse has a regular meter but no rhyme, commonly used by Shakespeare.

Question 15

The Greek term for the three rules of drama (time, place, and action) is the

  • A. Dramatic unities
  • B. Tragic triad
  • C. Aristotelian unities
  • D. Stage conventions
Answer: C. Aristotelian unities
Explanation: These unities require a play to have one main action, in one location, over one day.

Question 16

A poem of five lines with a specific AABBA rhyme scheme is a

  • A. Haiku
  • B. Limerick
  • C. Sonnet
  • D. Quatrain
Answer: B. Limerick
Explanation: Limericks are short, humorous poems with a bouncy rhythm.

Question 17

An address to an object or an idea as if it were alive and present is an

  • A. Apostrophe
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Analogy
  • D. Allegory
Answer: A. Apostrophe
Explanation: In an apostrophe, the speaker speaks to someone dead, absent, or to an inanimate object.

Question 18

A novel that depicts the adventures of a roguish hero of low social class is a

  • A. Gothic novel
  • B. Picaresque novel
  • C. Historical novel
  • D. Epistolary novel
Answer: B. Picaresque novel
Explanation: Picaresque novels are episodic and usually told in the first person.

Question 19

Which figure of speech is seen in the expression He is a citizen of no mean city?

  • A. Hyperbole
  • B. Litotes
  • C. Irony
  • D. Oxymoron
Answer: B. Litotes
Explanation: Litotes uses a negative to affirm a positive, meaning he comes from a very great city.

Question 20

The pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a poem is its

  • A. Rhyme
  • B. Meter
  • C. Stanza
  • D. Couplet
Answer: B. Meter
Explanation: Meter provides the rhythmic structure of the poetic lines.

Question 21

The introduction of the characters and the basic situation at the start of a story is the

  • A. Climax
  • B. Exposition
  • C. Denouement
  • D. Rising action
Answer: B. Exposition
Explanation: Exposition provides essential background information needed to understand the story.

Question 22

A type of irony where the opposite of what is expected happens is

  • A. Verbal irony
  • B. Situational irony
  • C. Dramatic irony
  • D. Sarcasm
Answer: B. Situational irony
Explanation: Situational irony occurs when the outcome is the reverse of what was intended or expected.

Question 23

The repeating of sounds at the beginning of words in a line is

  • A. Consonance
  • B. Alliteration
  • C. Assonance
  • D. Onomatopoeia
Answer: B. Alliteration
Explanation: Alliteration is a sound device used to create rhythm or mood.

Question 24

A speech made by a character after the main action of a play has ended is the

  • A. Prologue
  • B. Epilogue
  • C. Monologue
  • D. Soliloquy
Answer: B. Epilogue
Explanation: An epilogue often provides a final comment or moral to the play.

Question 25

The term for measuring the meter in a line of poetry is

  • A. Verse
  • B. Scansion
  • C. Prosody
  • D. Rhyme
Answer: B. Scansion
Explanation: Scansion involves marking the stressed and unstressed syllables to find the meter.

Question 26

Which figure of speech is an indirect reference to a famous person or event?

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Allegory
  • D. Analogy
Answer: B. Allusion
Explanation: Allusions rely on the reader knowledge of history, culture, or other literature.

Question 27

A poem that has three stanzas and is usually written for a specific person or event is an

  • A. Ode
  • B. Sonnet
  • C. Elegy
  • D. Lyric
Answer: A. Ode
Explanation: Odes are formal, ceremonial poems that praise their subject.

Question 28

The use of an object to represent a larger, more abstract idea is

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Symbolism
  • C. Irony
  • D. Personification
Answer: B. Symbolism
Explanation: Symbols like a dove for peace are used to deepen the meaning of a work.

Question 29

A story that has both a literal and a symbolic meaning is an

  • A. Allusion
  • B. Allegory
  • C. Analogy
  • D. Anecdote
Answer: B. Allegory
Explanation: Allegories use characters and events to represent hidden meanings or moral lessons.

Question 30

The narrative point of view that uses pronouns like I and me is

  • A. First person
  • B. Second person
  • C. Third person
  • D. Omniscient
Answer: A. First person
Explanation: The narrator is a character within the story telling it from their own perspective.

Question 31

A figure of speech where two opposite ideas are joined to create an effect is an

  • A. Oxymoron
  • B. Antithesis
  • C. Metaphor
  • D. Simile
Answer: B. Antithesis
Explanation: Antithesis emphasizes contrast, such as Speech is silver, but silence is gold.

Question 32

The turning point in a story where the main character fate changes is the

  • A. Peripeteia
  • B. Anagnorisis
  • C. Hamartia
  • D. Catharsis
Answer: A. Peripeteia
Explanation: Peripeteia is a sudden reversal of fortune or change in circumstances.

Question 33

A character who remains the same throughout the entire story is a

  • A. Dynamic character
  • B. Static character
  • C. Round character
  • D. Complex character
Answer: B. Static character
Explanation: Static characters do not undergo significant personal growth or change.

Question 34

A literary genre that uses exaggerated characters and improbable situations for comedy is

  • A. Tragedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Satire
  • D. Epic
Answer: B. Farce
Explanation: Farce is a type of low comedy designed to provoke laughter through slapstick and absurdity.

Question 35

A poetic line with five feet (ten syllables) is called a

  • A. Tetrameter
  • B. Pentameter
  • C. Hexameter
  • D. Trimeter
Answer: B. Pentameter
Explanation: Iambic pentameter is the most common meter in English poetry.

Question 36

The central character of a tragedy whose downfall is caused by a fatal flaw is the

  • A. Tragic hero
  • B. Antagonist
  • C. Villain
  • D. Foil
Answer: A. Tragic hero
Explanation: The tragic hero usually has noble traits but is ruined by hamartia.

Question 37

The atmosphere or emotional setting created by a writer is the

  • A. Tone
  • B. Mood
  • C. Style
  • D. Theme
Answer: B. Mood
Explanation: Mood refers to the feelings the reader has while reading the text.

Question 38

A short, witty statement that expresses a clever or satirical thought is an

  • A. Epigram
  • B. Epitaph
  • C. Eulogy
  • D. Epic
Answer: A. Epigram
Explanation: An epigram is a brief, pointed, and often paradoxical saying.

Question 39

The sequence of events leading up to the climax of a story is the

  • A. Exposition
  • B. Rising action
  • C. Falling action
  • D. Resolution
Answer: B. Rising action
Explanation: Rising action builds tension and develops the conflict toward the peak of the story.

Question 40

Which figure of speech is used in The kettle is boiling?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Metonymy
  • C. Synecdoche
  • D. Personification
Answer: B. Metonymy
Explanation: The kettle (container) is used to refer to the water (content) inside it.

Question 41

A poem that consists of three unrhymed lines with 5, 7, and 5 syllables is a

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Haiku
  • C. Limerick
  • D. Tanka
Answer: B. Haiku
Explanation: Haiku is a traditional form of Japanese poetry focused on nature.

Question 42

The choice of words and style of expression used by an author is

  • A. Syntax
  • B. Diction
  • C. Tone
  • D. Mood
Answer: B. Diction
Explanation: Diction can be formal, informal, poetic, or colloquial.

Question 43

The point in a play where the hero realizes their mistake or true identity is

  • A. Hamartia
  • B. Anagnorisis
  • C. Peripeteia
  • D. Catharsis
Answer: B. Anagnorisis
Explanation: Anagnorisis is the moment of recognition or discovery in a drama.

Question 44

A poem written in memory of a deceased person that is often inscribed on a tombstone is an

  • A. Elegy
  • B. Epitaph
  • C. Eulogy
  • D. Epigram
Answer: B. Epitaph
Explanation: An epitaph is a short text honoring a deceased person, often found on their monument.

Question 45

The repeating of vowel sounds in a line of poetry is

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Assonance
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Onomatopoeia
Answer: B. Assonance
Explanation: Assonance is the repetition of vowel sounds to create internal rhyming within phrases.

Question 46

A stanza of three lines is called a

  • A. Couplet
  • B. Tercet
  • C. Quatrain
  • D. Sestet
Answer: B. Tercet
Explanation: Tercets can be rhymed or unrhymed, such as in the form of terza rima.

Question 47

A humorous poem that makes fun of a specific person or work is a

  • A. Satire
  • B. Parody
  • C. Tragedy
  • D. Epic
Answer: B. Parody
Explanation: Parody imitates the style of another work for comic effect or ridicule.

Question 48

The resolution or wrapping up of the plot at the end of a story is the

  • A. Climax
  • B. Denouement
  • C. Exposition
  • D. Rising action
Answer: B. Denouement
Explanation: This is where all the mysteries are solved and the conflict is finally settled.

Question 49

A type of play that combines elements of both tragedy and comedy is a

  • A. Melodrama
  • B. Tragi comedy
  • C. Farce
  • D. Satire
Answer: B. Tragi comedy
Explanation: Tragi comedies often have a serious theme but end happily or contain lighthearted moments.

Question 50

Which figure of speech is an overstatement used to emphasize a point?

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Hyperbole
  • C. Metaphor
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Hyperbole
Explanation: Hyperbole is a deliberate exaggeration used for emphasis or dramatic effect.
