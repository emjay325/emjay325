Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

Question 1

A character who remains unchanged throughout the course of a narrative is known as a

  • A. Dynamic character
  • B. Static character
  • C. Round character
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: B. Static character
Explanation: Static characters do not undergo any significant internal change or growth during the story.

Question 2

The literary device that uses a concrete object to represent an abstract idea is

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Simile
  • C. Symbolism
  • D. Personification
Answer: C. Symbolism
Explanation: Symbolism occurs when an author uses a physical object to suggest a deeper or more complex meaning.

Question 3

A short poem that consists of only two lines is called a

  • A. Tercet
  • B. Quatrain
  • C. Couplet
  • D. Sestet
Answer: C. Couplet
Explanation: A couplet is a pair of successive lines of verse, typically rhyming and having the same meter.

Question 4

The purging of emotions through the experience of a tragedy is known as

  • A. Hubris
  • B. Hamartia
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Anagnorisis
Answer: C. Catharsis
Explanation: Catharsis is the emotional release felt by the audience at the end of a tragic play.

Question 5

A reference within a literary work to another famous work of literature or history is an

  • A. Allegory
  • B. Allusion
  • C. Alliteration
  • D. Apostrophe
Answer: B. Allusion
Explanation: Allusion is a brief and indirect reference to a person, place, thing, or idea of historical or literary significance.

Question 6

The pattern of end rhymes in a poem is referred to as the

  • A. Rhythm
  • B. Meter
  • C. Rhyme scheme
  • D. Stanza
Answer: C. Rhyme scheme
Explanation: Rhyme scheme is the formal arrangement of rhymes in a stanza or throughout a poem.

Question 7

Which figure of speech is used in the phrase The iron tongue of midnight told twelve?

  • A. Simile
  • B. Personification
  • C. Oxymoron
  • D. Hyperbole
Answer: B. Personification
Explanation: Midnight and a bell are given a human characteristic (a tongue) to describe the striking of the hour.

Question 8

A humorous play that uses exaggerated situations and physical comedy is a

  • A. Tragedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Melodrama
  • D. Epic
Answer: B. Farce
Explanation: Farce is a comedy that depends on ridiculous situations rather than character development.

Question 9

The major division within a play is called an

  • A. Act
  • B. Scene
  • C. Episode
  • D. Chapter
Answer: A. Act
Explanation: Plays are divided into acts, which are further subdivided into scenes.

Question 10

A narrator who tells the story from the perspective of I is using

  • A. First person point of view
  • B. Second person point of view
  • C. Third person point of view
  • D. Omniscient point of view
Answer: A. First person point of view
Explanation: In first person narration, the narrator is a character in the story and uses personal pronouns.

Question 11

Which of the following is a fixed form of Japanese poetry with seventeen syllables?

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Haiku
  • C. Limerick
  • D. Villanelle
Answer: B. Haiku
Explanation: A Haiku consists of three lines with a five, seven, five syllable structure.

Question 12

The excessive pride or self confidence that leads a tragic hero to ignore warnings is

  • A. Hamartia
  • B. Hubris
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Hubris
Explanation: Hubris is a specific type of tragic flaw involving overwhelming arrogance.

Question 13

A long speech delivered by a character who is alone on stage is a

  • A. Monologue
  • B. Dialogue
  • C. Soliloquy
  • D. Aside
Answer: C. Soliloquy
Explanation: A soliloquy is used to reveal a character inner thoughts directly to the audience while alone.

Question 14

The central message or underlying meaning of a literary work is its

  • A. Plot
  • B. Setting
  • C. Theme
  • D. Tone
Answer: C. Theme
Explanation: Theme is the main idea or insight about life that the author conveys through the work.

Question 15

The choice of words and style of expression used by an author is known as

  • A. Syntax
  • B. Diction
  • C. Mood
  • D. Theme
Answer: B. Diction
Explanation: Diction refers to the specific vocabulary and language level chosen by a writer.

Question 16

Which figure of speech involves an intentional exaggeration for emphasis?

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Hyperbole
  • C. Metonymy
  • D. Oxymoron
Answer: B. Hyperbole
Explanation: Hyperbole is an overstatement used to create a strong impression or for comic effect.

Question 17

A stanza consisting of four lines is called a

  • A. Couplet
  • B. Tercet
  • C. Quatrain
  • D. Octave
Answer: C. Quatrain
Explanation: Quatrains are the most common stanza form in English poetry.

Question 18

The substitution of a mild or pleasant expression for a harsh or offensive one is a

  • A. Malapropism
  • B. Euphemism
  • C. Pun
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Euphemism
Explanation: Euphemisms are used to soften the impact of sensitive or unpleasant topics.

Question 19

A narrative technique that interrupts the chronological order to show past events is a

  • A. Foreshadowing
  • B. Flashback
  • C. Suspense
  • D. Climax
Answer: B. Flashback
Explanation: Flashbacks provide background information by moving the story back in time.

Question 20

The time and place where the action of a story occurs is the

  • A. Plot
  • B. Atmosphere
  • C. Setting
  • D. Context
Answer: C. Setting
Explanation: Setting establishes the physical and temporal environment of a literary work.

Question 21

A figure of speech where a part is used to represent the whole is

  • A. Metonymy
  • B. Synecdoche
  • C. Paradox
  • D. Simile
Answer: B. Synecdoche
Explanation: An example is using hands to refer to workers.

Question 22

The repetition of vowel sounds within a line of poetry is

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Assonance
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Onomatopoeia
Answer: B. Assonance
Explanation: Assonance creates internal rhyming through similar vowel sounds.

Question 23

An introduction to a book or play is called a

  • A. Prologue
  • B. Epilogue
  • C. Appendix
  • D. Denouement
Answer: A. Prologue
Explanation: A prologue provides necessary background or sets the mood before the main action.

Question 24

A figure of speech in which contradictory terms appear side by side is an

  • A. Paradox
  • B. Oxymoron
  • C. Irony
  • D. Metaphor
Answer: B. Oxymoron
Explanation: Examples include deafening silence and bitter sweet.

Question 25

The point of highest emotional intensity in a plot is the

  • A. Rising action
  • B. Falling action
  • C. Climax
  • D. Exposition
Answer: C. Climax
Explanation: The climax is the turning point where the conflict reaches its peak.

Question 26

Which figure of speech is used in The wind howled through the night?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Personification
  • C. Simile
  • D. Synecdoche
Answer: B. Personification
Explanation: Giving a human action (howling) to the wind is personification.

Question 27

A long narrative poem celebrating the deeds of a hero is an

  • A. Ode
  • B. Epic
  • C. Elegy
  • D. Sonnet
Answer: B. Epic
Explanation: Epics involve heroic figures and legendary events.

Question 28

When the audience knows something that the characters do not, it is called

  • A. Verbal irony
  • B. Situational irony
  • C. Dramatic irony
  • D. Sarcasm
Answer: C. Dramatic irony
Explanation: Dramatic irony creates suspense because the audience can anticipate outcomes characters cannot.

Question 29

The resolution or final outcome of a story is the

  • A. Climax
  • B. Denouement
  • C. Rising action
  • D. Conflict
Answer: B. Denouement
Explanation: Denouement is the final part of a plot where the strands are drawn together.

Question 30

A play that ends in the death or ruin of the main character is a

  • A. Comedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Tragedy
  • D. Satire
Answer: C. Tragedy
Explanation: Tragedy involves serious themes and a disastrous conclusion for the protagonist.

Question 31

A poem written to mourn the death of a person is an

  • A. Ode
  • B. Elegy
  • C. Sonnet
  • D. Ballad
Answer: B. Elegy
Explanation: An elegy is a formal poem expressing sorrow for someone who has died.

Question 32

The attitude of a writer toward the subject or audience is the

  • A. Mood
  • B. Tone
  • C. Style
  • D. Diction
Answer: B. Tone
Explanation: Tone is conveyed through word choice and details, reflecting the author feelings.

Question 33

A brief story told to illustrate a point or provide a moral lesson is a

  • A. Novel
  • B. Anecdote
  • C. Parable
  • D. Epic
Answer: C. Parable
Explanation: Parables are short, allegorical stories that teach moral or religious lessons.

Question 34

The repetition of initial consonant sounds in a line of poetry is

  • A. Assonance
  • B. Alliteration
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Onomatopoeia
Answer: B. Alliteration
Explanation: Alliteration is the repetition of the same letter or sound at the beginning of adjacent words.

Question 35

A character who provides a contrast to the main character is a

  • A. Foil
  • B. Protagonist
  • C. Antagonist
  • D. Hero
Answer: A. Foil
Explanation: A foil helps to highlight the specific traits of another character through comparison.

Question 36

The use of light and humorous scenes in a serious play is called

  • A. Satire
  • B. Comic relief
  • C. Irony
  • D. Farce
Answer: B. Comic relief
Explanation: Comic relief breaks the tension in a serious or tragic work.

Question 37

A play intended to teach a moral lesson is described as

  • A. Didactic
  • B. Satirical
  • C. Epic
  • D. Lyric
Answer: A. Didactic
Explanation: Didactic literature aims to instruct or convey moral or ethical principles.

Question 38

Which figure of speech is an address to an absent person or an inanimate object?

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Apostrophe
  • C. Metaphor
  • D. Personification
Answer: B. Apostrophe
Explanation: In apostrophe, the speaker speaks directly to someone or something that cannot respond.

Question 39

A poem of fourteen lines with a specific rhyme scheme is a

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Ode
  • C. Villanelle
  • D. Haiku
Answer: A. Sonnet
Explanation: Sonnets are fixed verse forms usually written in iambic pentameter.

Question 40

The struggle between opposing forces in a story is the

  • A. Theme
  • B. Plot
  • C. Conflict
  • D. Resolution
Answer: C. Conflict
Explanation: Conflict drives the narrative and can be internal or external.

Question 41

A figure of speech where a name is replaced by something closely associated with it is

  • A. Synecdoche
  • B. Metonymy
  • C. Paradox
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Metonymy
Explanation: An example is using the White House to refer to the American presidency.

Question 42

The error of judgment that leads to the downfall of a tragic hero is

  • A. Hubris
  • B. Hamartia
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Hamartia
Explanation: Hamartia is the tragic flaw that causes the protagonist inevitable ruin.

Question 43

A narrative in which characters and events represent abstract ideas is an

  • A. Allusion
  • B. Allegory
  • C. Anecdote
  • D. Epic
Answer: B. Allegory
Explanation: Allegories have both literal and symbolic levels of meaning.

Question 44

Which figure of speech is seen in Parting is such sweet sorrow?

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Oxymoron
  • C. Personification
  • D. Simile
Answer: B. Oxymoron
Explanation: Sweet and sorrow are contradictory terms used together for effect.

Question 45

A short speech directed to the audience that characters on stage cannot hear is an

  • A. Soliloquy
  • B. Aside
  • C. Monologue
  • D. Dialogue
Answer: B. Aside
Explanation: An aside is a dramatic convention used to share private thoughts with the audience.

Question 46

The use of words whose sound suggests their meaning is

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Onomatopoeia
  • C. Assonance
  • D. Consonance
Answer: B. Onomatopoeia
Explanation: Words like buzz and hiss are onomatopoeic.

Question 47

A character who opposes the protagonist is the

  • A. Hero
  • B. Antagonist
  • C. Foil
  • D. Stock character
Answer: B. Antagonist
Explanation: The antagonist creates conflict for the main character.

Question 48

A poem that expresses personal emotions of a speaker is a

  • A. Narrative
  • B. Lyric
  • C. Epic
  • D. Ballad
Answer: B. Lyric
Explanation: Lyric poems are typically short and musical in quality.

Question 49

An unexpected event that saves a hopeless situation in a play is

  • A. Denouement
  • B. Deus ex machina
  • C. Climax
  • D. Prologue
Answer: B. Deus ex machina
Explanation: This is a plot device where an unsolvable problem is suddenly resolved by an improbable intervention.

Question 50

The dictionary meaning of a word is its

  • A. Connotation
  • B. Denotation
  • C. Syntax
  • D. Tone
Answer: B. Denotation
Explanation: Denotation is the literal meaning of a word, as opposed to its emotional associations (connotation).
