Sample WAEC-style Practice Questions (Original Content)

WAEC Style

Question 1

A figure of speech which makes a comparison between two unlike things using as or like is known as

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Simile
  • C. Personification
  • D. Oxymoron
Answer: B. Simile
Explanation: A simile explicitly compares two things using words such as like or as.

Question 2

The main character in a play or novel is often referred to as the

  • A. Antagonist
  • B. Villain
  • C. Protagonist
  • D. Narrator
Answer: C. Protagonist
Explanation: The protagonist is the central figure or hero in a literary work.

Question 3

A speech made by a character alone on stage to reveal their inner thoughts is a

  • A. Dialogue
  • B. Monologue
  • C. Soliloquy
  • D. Prologue
Answer: C. Soliloquy
Explanation: A soliloquy is a dramatic device where a character speaks to themselves, revealing thoughts to the audience.

Question 4

The use of a word whose sound suggests its meaning is called

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Onomatopoeia
  • C. Assonance
  • D. Pun
Answer: B. Onomatopoeia
Explanation: Words like hiss or bang are examples of onomatopoeia because they imitate sounds.

Question 5

A poem of fourteen lines with a specific rhyme scheme is a

  • A. Lyric
  • B. Ballad
  • C. Sonnet
  • D. Ode
Answer: C. Sonnet
Explanation: A sonnet is a fixed form of poetry consisting of exactly fourteen lines.

Question 6

The resolution of a plot in a tragedy is also known as the

  • A. Climax
  • B. Denouement
  • C. Catastrophe
  • D. Exposition
Answer: C. Catastrophe
Explanation: In tragedy, the final disastrous event is called the catastrophe.

Question 7

Giving human qualities to non human objects is known as

  • A. Hyperbole
  • B. Personification
  • C. Irony
  • D. Paradox
Answer: B. Personification
Explanation: Personification treats inanimate objects or animals as if they have human feelings or actions.

Question 8

A play that ends on a happy note is generally classified as a

  • A. Tragedy
  • B. Farce
  • C. Comedy
  • D. Melodrama
Answer: C. Comedy
Explanation: Comedies are designed to be amusing and usually end with a positive resolution like a wedding.

Question 9

The pattern of rhymes at the end of the lines of a poem is the

  • A. Rhythm
  • B. Meter
  • C. Rhyme scheme
  • D. Stanza
Answer: C. Rhyme scheme
Explanation: The rhyme scheme is the formal arrangement of rhymes in a stanza or a whole poem.

Question 10

A deliberate exaggeration for emphasis is called

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Hyperbole
  • C. Euphemism
  • D. Metonymy
Answer: B. Hyperbole
Explanation: Hyperbole is an overstatement used to create a strong impression or emphasize a point.

Question 11

The time and place in which a story happens is the

  • A. Plot
  • B. Setting
  • C. Theme
  • D. Atmosphere
Answer: B. Setting
Explanation: Setting provides the geographical location and the time period of the narrative.

Question 12

A character who provides a contrast to the protagonist is a

  • A. Hero
  • B. Foil
  • C. Peer
  • D. Mentor
Answer: B. Foil
Explanation: A foil highlights the qualities of the main character through contrast.

Question 13

The central idea or message of a literary work is its

  • A. Subject
  • B. Plot
  • C. Theme
  • D. Conflict
Answer: C. Theme
Explanation: The theme is the underlying meaning or insight into life expressed by the author.

Question 14

Which figure of speech is used in the phrase The heavy clouds wept?

  • A. Synecdoche
  • B. Personification
  • C. Paradox
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Personification
Explanation: Attributing the act of weeping to clouds gives them human characteristics.

Question 15

A short poem expressing the personal emotions or feelings of the speaker is a

  • A. Narrative
  • B. Epic
  • C. Lyric
  • D. Ballad
Answer: C. Lyric
Explanation: Lyric poems are typically short and focus on the internal state of the speaker.

Question 16

The choice and use of words in writing is known as

  • A. Syntax
  • B. Diction
  • C. Tone
  • D. Style
Answer: B. Diction
Explanation: Diction refers to the vocabulary used by an author to convey a specific mood or meaning.

Question 17

An address to an absent person or a personified object is called

  • A. Apostrophe
  • B. Epilogue
  • C. Eulogy
  • D. Allusion
Answer: A. Apostrophe
Explanation: In an apostrophe, the speaker speaks directly to someone or something that cannot answer.

Question 18

A play or story intended to teach a moral lesson is

  • A. Satire
  • B. Didactic
  • C. Allegory
  • D. Parody
Answer: B. Didactic
Explanation: Didactic literature aims primarily to instruct or provide moral guidance.

Question 19

The repetition of vowel sounds in a line of poetry is

  • A. Alliteration
  • B. Assonance
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Onomatopoeia
Answer: B. Assonance
Explanation: Assonance is the repetition of similar vowel sounds within nearby words.

Question 20

The point of highest tension or interest in a plot is the

  • A. Falling action
  • B. Exposition
  • C. Climax
  • D. Rising action
Answer: C. Climax
Explanation: The climax is the turning point where the conflict reaches its peak.

Question 21

The use of light and funny scenes to interrupt a serious or tragic dramatic work is

  • A. Tragic irony
  • B. Comic relief
  • C. Satire
  • D. Farce
Answer: B. Comic relief
Explanation: Comic relief provides the audience a moment of relaxation from intense emotional tension.

Question 22

Which of these is a long narrative poem celebrating the deeds of a hero?

  • A. Sonnet
  • B. Epic
  • C. Haiku
  • D. Elegy
Answer: B. Epic
Explanation: Epics like the Iliad or Beowulf focus on large scale heroic adventures.

Question 23

A hint or clue of what is to come later in a story is

  • A. Flashback
  • B. Foreshadowing
  • C. Suspense
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Foreshadowing
Explanation: Foreshadowing builds anticipation and prepares the reader for future events.

Question 24

A poem written to mourn the death of a person is an

  • A. Ode
  • B. Elegy
  • C. Epigram
  • D. Epitaph
Answer: B. Elegy
Explanation: An elegy is a sorrowful poem, often used as a lament for the dead.

Question 25

The words spoken by characters in a play is the

  • A. Prologue
  • B. Epilogue
  • C. Dialogue
  • D. Script
Answer: C. Dialogue
Explanation: Dialogue consists of the conversational exchange between characters.

Question 26

Which figure of speech expresses a bitter or mocking remark?

  • A. Sarcasm
  • B. Euphemism
  • C. Allusion
  • D. Metaphor
Answer: A. Sarcasm
Explanation: Sarcasm uses irony to mock or convey contempt.

Question 27

A stanza of four lines is called a

  • A. Couplet
  • B. Tercet
  • C. Quatrain
  • D. Sestet
Answer: C. Quatrain
Explanation: Quatrains are the most common stanza form in English poetry.

Question 28

A reference to a well known person, place, or event in history or literature is an

  • A. Allegory
  • B. Alliteration
  • C. Allusion
  • D. Analogy
Answer: C. Allusion
Explanation: Allusion allows an author to enrich a work by linking it to other cultural knowledge.

Question 29

The person telling the story in a novel is the

  • A. Author
  • B. Narrator
  • C. Persona
  • D. Protagonist
Answer: B. Narrator
Explanation: The narrator is the voice or character who conveys the story to the reader.

Question 30

A struggle between opposing forces in a story is the

  • A. Climax
  • B. Conflict
  • C. Suspense
  • D. Resolution
Answer: B. Conflict
Explanation: Conflict can be internal (man versus self) or external (man versus man or nature).

Question 31

A mild or pleasant word used instead of one that is harsh or offensive is a

  • A. Litotes
  • B. Euphemism
  • C. Oxymoron
  • D. Pun
Answer: B. Euphemism
Explanation: Passing away is a euphemism for dying.

Question 32

Two lines of verse that rhyme and have the same meter are a

  • A. Sestet
  • B. Octave
  • C. Couplet
  • D. Quatrain
Answer: C. Couplet
Explanation: A couplet consists of two successive rhyming lines.

Question 33

The use of humor, irony, or ridicule to expose and criticize human vice is

  • A. Satire
  • B. Farce
  • C. Parody
  • D. Tragedy
Answer: A. Satire
Explanation: Satire is often used in literature to bring about social or political change.

Question 34

A statement that appears self contradictory but reveals a truth is a

  • A. Metaphor
  • B. Paradox
  • C. Oxymoron
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Paradox
Explanation: An example of a paradox is the beginning of the end.

Question 35

The attitude of the author toward the subject or audience is the

  • A. Mood
  • B. Tone
  • C. Style
  • D. Diction
Answer: B. Tone
Explanation: Tone can be formal, informal, serious, comic, sarcastic, or sad.

Question 36

An introduction to a play or a long poem is the

  • A. Epilogue
  • B. Prologue
  • C. Preface
  • D. Foreword
Answer: B. Prologue
Explanation: A prologue sets the scene or provides background information before the main action begins.

Question 37

When a character speaks to the audience and the other characters on stage are not supposed to hear, it is an

  • A. Aside
  • B. Soliloquy
  • C. Monologue
  • D. Interior monologue
Answer: A. Aside
Explanation: An aside is a brief comment directed to the audience for dramatic effect.

Question 38

Literature that is based on the imagination and not on facts is

  • A. Non fiction
  • B. Biography
  • C. Fiction
  • D. Autobiography
Answer: C. Fiction
Explanation: Fiction includes novels, short stories, and plays that are invented by the author.

Question 39

The repetition of initial consonant sounds in a line of poetry is

  • A. Assonance
  • B. Alliteration
  • C. Consonance
  • D. Rhythm
Answer: B. Alliteration
Explanation: An example of alliteration is Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.

Question 40

A character who does not change throughout the story is a

  • A. Dynamic character
  • B. Static character
  • C. Round character
  • D. Flat character
Answer: B. Static character
Explanation: Static characters remain the same from the beginning to the end of the narrative.

Question 41

The error of judgment or character flaw that leads to the downfall of a tragic hero is

  • A. Hubris
  • B. Hamartia
  • C. Nemesis
  • D. Catharsis
Answer: B. Hamartia
Explanation: Hamartia is the Greek term for the tragic flaw.

Question 42

Which literary genre uses actors to perform stories on a stage?

  • A. Poetry
  • B. Prose
  • C. Drama
  • D. Non fiction
Answer: C. Drama
Explanation: Drama is intended to be performed by actors for an audience.

Question 43

A story that uses symbols to convey a deeper moral or political meaning is an

  • A. Allusion
  • B. Allegory
  • C. Anecdote
  • D. Autobiography
Answer: B. Allegory
Explanation: George Orwell s Animal Farm is a famous political allegory.

Question 44

The feeling or atmosphere a writer creates for the reader is the

  • A. Tone
  • B. Mood
  • C. Theme
  • D. Voice
Answer: B. Mood
Explanation: Mood is the emotional response that the writer pulls out of the reader.

Question 45

A play that mocks a serious work in a light or humorous way is a

  • A. Satire
  • B. Parody
  • C. Tragedy
  • D. Melodrama
Answer: B. Parody
Explanation: Parody imitates the style of a particular writer or genre with deliberate exaggeration for comic effect.

Question 46

Excessive pride which often leads to the downfall of a hero is

  • A. Hamartia
  • B. Hubris
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Irony
Answer: B. Hubris
Explanation: Hubris is a specific type of flaw involving overwhelming arrogance or self confidence.

Question 47

An imitation of a serious work using low or vulgar language is

  • A. Burlesque
  • B. Comedy
  • C. Farce
  • D. Satire
Answer: A. Burlesque
Explanation: Burlesque is a style of entertainment that makes fun of a serious subject by treating it in a ridiculous way.

Question 48

The purging or release of emotions like pity and fear by the audience at the end of a tragedy is

  • A. Hubris
  • B. Hamartia
  • C. Catharsis
  • D. Climax
Answer: C. Catharsis
Explanation: Catharsis is the emotional cleansing experienced by the audience of a tragedy.

Question 49

A figure of speech in which two contradictory terms are combined is an

  • A. Paradox
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Oxymoron
  • D. Pun
Answer: C. Oxymoron
Explanation: Examples include bittersweet or deafening silence.

Question 50

A story that explains the origin of the world or natural phenomena is a

  • A. Legend
  • B. Fable
  • C. Myth
  • D. Folktale
Answer: C. Myth
Explanation: Myths are traditional stories, often involving supernatural beings, that explain how things came to be.
